# How Warmup's practice voters talk: the rules

Every rule here came from Robert testing the app on his phone. Each one says what went wrong, what the voter does now and where the rule is enforced, so it can't quietly come undone. Read this before changing a voter's instructions (ai/scenarios.js), the voter's server code (ai/worker.js) or the door screens.

**How a new piece of feedback gets saved (Robert, Nov 16, 2026):**
1. **Enforce it in code when you can.** A rule in the server's code holds every time; a rule only in the AI's instructions can drift.
2. **Replay it.** Add the exact conversation Robert flagged to `tests/voter-replays.mjs`, with the check it must pass. A unit test with a stand-in model goes in `tests/turn.test.mjs` when the rule is in code.
3. **Write it here** in plain words, with his before and after.
4. **Check the live voter.** Run `WARMUP_AI_URL=https://warmup-ai.robertrgambhir.workers.dev node tests/voter-replays.mjs 3` after any change to a voter. A scheduled task runs it every week, so a change in the AI model itself gets caught too.

## The bar for every practice door

- **All three, always (Robert, Nov 15):** the voter answers at once or close to it, over a real back-and-forth, and to what the canvasser actually said. "Boy, it sure is sunny today" gets a reply about the weather, not "Who are you with?" *Enforced:* the streamed voter (/turn), the 1 s pause rule on the page, smallTalk in worker.js (small talk always gets a reply before the voter's question), the live replays.

## Lesson 1 (Sofia's 1st intro and Kevin's 2nd intro)

1. **The two intros are the same experience, graded the same way (Robert, Nov 15).** A full back-and-forth at a door both times, graded on the same four parts. *Enforced:* sofia_intro copies kevin_intro's rubric, facts and wrong claims (scenarios.js).
2. **A friendly first door, not a test of everything (Robert, Nov 15).** Short, plain lines that are easy to answer. Before: "I'm planning to vote for her Tuesday morning, same as always" (sounds like Maya ran before). After: "Oh, Maya Kaur! Yes, I'm voting for her." *Enforced:* the voters' reactions (scenarios.js); the replays check line length and "same as always."
3. **Never ask what the canvasser couldn't know (Robert, Nov 15).** Before: "What time do the polls open Tuesday?" After: no questions about polling hours, places or Maya's positions. "Anything else?" gets "No, I'm all set, thanks for volunteering." *Enforced:* scenarios.js; the replays check for poll questions.
4. **Ask only for a part that's really missing, and ask it plainly (Robert, Nov 15 and 16).** The server works out which parts were said (name, Maya Kaur, why they came) and writes the question itself; the AI adds only a few words of reaction, dropped if odd. Before: he said "the election" and Sofia asked "What's this about?"; then "Oh, hi Robert. Yes, I know about Tuesday. Oh, nice. Who are you volunteering with?"; then, asked twice, "Sorry, I'm a bit confused by what you're after. Your name first? Then you're volunteering for someone?" After: "Oh, hi there. Sorry, and you are?", then once more "Sorry, I didn't catch your name.", then she moves on to the next missing part. She only knows what she has been told (Robert, Nov 17): "Hi, I'm Robert, um, I'm" got "Who are you volunteering with?" though he never said he was volunteering. Now a name and nothing else gets "What can I do for you?", and "volunteering" is in her question only once the canvasser has said it ("And who are you with?" otherwise). *Enforced:* introParts, introPlan and cleanReaction in worker.js; turn.test.mjs; the replays.
5. **At most four of the canvasser's lines; the voter wraps up once the four parts are done (Robert, Nov 15).** *Enforced:* wrapAt 3 and hardStop 4 (scenarios.js), the page's cap.

## Every door

6. **A goodbye ends the door (Robert, Nov 15).** When the voter says goodbye, the door closes and grading starts; the canvasser gets no extra turn. A line that arrives after her goodbye gets "Bye now!" with no AI call. The canvasser's goodbye works the same way: she says goodbye back and the door closes, even if a part of the introduction is still missing (165-10 and 165-11; before, she answered "Thanks, bye now!" with "You too, bye! And what brings you by?"). *Enforced:* FAREWELL and saidBye in worker.js, and the missing-part question is skipped after a goodbye; turn.test.mjs; the replays.
7. **The voter never steps out of character (Robert, Nov 15).** Before (after his "You too goodnight"): "I can't write Sofia's next line in this exchange, because the conversation already reached its goodbye..." After: any line that talks about lines, turns, the conversation, practicing or being an AI is never shown or spoken, and the door ends with a plain goodbye. *Enforced:* outOfCharacter in worker.js; turn.test.mjs; the replays.
8. **The goodbye isn't the canvasser's to miss when the voter closed the door (Nov 15).** *Enforced:* the close item's definition (scenarios.js).
9. **Spoken words get punctuation, never changed words (Robert, Nov 15).** *Enforced:* /tidy and sameWords in worker.js; turn.test.mjs.

4b. **Answer the volunteer's question before asking hers (Robert, Nov 17).** Before: "Hi, I'm Robert, um, I'm volunteering, do you know that we have an election here next Tuesday" got "Oh, hi Robert! Who are you volunteering with?" After: "Oh, hi Robert! Yes, I know about Tuesday. Who are you volunteering with?" Lesson 1's voters are a little easier than real ones on purpose, so volunteers stay engaged. *Enforced:* smallTalk in worker.js (questions about the election, voting plans and "any questions?" are answered by the server if the AI skipped them; a greeting keeps its place); turn.test.mjs; the replays.

10b. **Wait for the volunteer; nervous people pause (Robert, Nov 17).** A line that sounds finished sends after 1.2 s of quiet; a line that stops mid-thought (ends on "um", "I'm", "and", "with" and the like, or is under three words) waits 2.8 s. Before: "Hi, I'm Robert, um, I'm" went to Sofia after 1 s. *Enforced:* waiPause in the app pages (version 172); test165.cjs.

## The issue practice (Sarah after Heating oil, Linda after Social Security; version 174, Nov 18)

These two voters were wired in overnight from the trainings' own voter sheets. **No person and no real model has talked to them yet:** they were built where the practice AI's key and a deployed copy of them didn't exist. Until `tests/issue-voters-live.mjs` has run against the live server and Robert has tried each door on his phone, treat everything below as untested with the real AI.

11. **The voter comes from the training, word for word.** Who she is, what she knows, how she reacts, her first line, the approved facts and the lines never to say are the voter sheet written with the training (issue-trainings repo, `voters/scenarios.js`), copied as it is. To change a voter, change the sheet there and run `ai/tools/sync-issue-trainings.py`. *Enforced:* `scenarios-issues.js` uses the sheet object itself and refuses a sheet that is missing a field; `tests/issues.test.mjs`.
12. **Her first line is the training's line.** Sarah: "I can't afford to fill my tank this winter." Linda: "Is my Social Security going to run out?" The page reads it from the server's card, so the two can't drift. *Enforced:* the door's `first` reads `WAI_PUB`; test174 checks the line on screen.
13. **The card is the four parts of the door answer:** agree, offer help (or explain the cause), give the choice, ask for the vote. "Stick to the facts" is judged off the card: a wrong claim shows as "Check this" and keeps the card from reading Excellent. *Enforced:* `checkSheet` (exactly four card rows), `tests/issues.test.mjs`.
14. **The door waits for a knock when it opens from a training.** The tap on "Practice the conversation" happens inside the training's frame, and a phone only starts a voice from a tap on the page itself. *Enforced:* `startIssue(..., { knock: true })`; test174.
15. **An issue practice is a whole conversation, not lesson 1's four lines.** The voter keeps going until she ends it; the server starts wrapping up at five of the canvasser's lines and stops at seven (the voter sheet's `wrapAt` and `hardStop`). Before: an independent review of version 174 found the door shut after the canvasser's fourth line, on her open question, because the page counted every AI door opened this way as lesson 1's. *Enforced:* `shortDoor()` in the app pages leaves issue doors out; test174 (F1).
16. **On an issue door the conduct check does not decide alone.** The checks for a threat, a come-on and a second insult were written for the short intro doors. In an issue conversation the same wording trips on ordinary sentences: "A cut like that would hurt you." "Maya should beat Jack Dufner on Tuesday." "I can text you the link, or I'll get you a paper copy." "I'll shoot you the link." "Are you single or is there a second income?" "It was a stupid war." Two passes at a narrower word list (174-2, 174-4) each failed an independent review in both directions: real threats got through and ordinary sentences still closed the door. A word list cannot tell "a cut like that would kill her" from a threat. So on Sarah's and Linda's doors:
    - **Plain:** a complete thought with no ordinary reading at a door ends it by the server's own hand, whatever the model says (main's note on this check says why the server makes sure: the model does not always comply). That is "I" threatening the voter with violence ("I'm gonna kill you." "I'll hurt you."), and the shapes of the project's own red-team lines: "... or we'll remember this house." "Someone should shoot him." "I'll report you to immigration." "... or I'll burn your house down." "You're hot." "Hey gorgeous," "What are you wearing under that?" and a second insult said to her face as a sentence of its own ("You're an idiot." then "You're a moron."). The words must end the sentence, and for "I'll hurt you" nothing earlier in it may hedge them: "or I'll shoot you their number," "or we'll wreck your kids' retirement too" and "I'm worried I'll hurt you more than help" go on, so they are judged, not ended. A put-down that is quoted or said of oneself ("he said, you're a moron," "I left the flyers in the car. Idiot.") is judged too. Seven of the nine red-team lines that Kevin's door ends by the server check end an issue door the same way. Two are judged: "I know where you live now. I'll be back tonight." (a neighbor with a form to drop off says the same words) and "Did you hear me? Idiot." (a lone "Idiot." is also what a nervous canvasser says of themselves).
    - **Suspect:** any other line the checks would end on Kevin's door (read with a phone's curly apostrophes made plain) is left to the voter. She is told the line tripped the check, that the check is crude, and to judge it at her door: a real threat, come-on or insult gets "that's not okay" and a goodbye; an ordinary sentence gets its normal answer. The server forces nothing.
    - So every line that ends Kevin's door is at least judged on Sarah's, and an ordinary sentence cannot close an issue door by a word list. **Every other door keeps its checks exactly as they were.**
    - **Not yet tried with the real model.** The live check (`tests/issue-voters-live.mjs`) asks it both ways after a deploy: two ordinary sentences that trip the check must leave the door open, a threat outside the plain wording must close it. The red-team set has not been run on these two doors.
    - *Enforced:* `conduct` (plain), `suspect` and `SUSPECT_NOTE` in worker.js (174-5, 174-7, 174-9); `tests/issues.test.mjs` with the sentences of five independent readers in `tests/issue-lines.mjs`: 310 ordinary sentences and 654 well-meant lines built on the trigger words, in seven written forms (the server ends none; 12 contrived ones it still ends are pinned), and 251 threats, come-ons and insult pairs (of the 240 that end Kevin's door the server ends 44 and the voter judges 196).
17. **A goodbye is a parting sentence of its own (issue doors).** Main's wording reads a goodbye word anywhere, which fits a voter who speaks one short sentence. Sarah and Linda speak two or three, and so does the canvasser: "So I can kiss $460 a month goodbye?", "Well, goodbye groceries.", "I see you've done your homework.", "Thanks for coming by. I'm listening." and "kids to take care of" are not goodbyes, and neither is a canvasser's "will I see you Tuesday." A wrong goodbye cuts a practice short; a missed one costs one more exchange, because the model's own end marker and the seven-line stop still close the door. So the rule leans hard toward staying open: before the goodbye phrase, back to the start of the sentence, only parting lead-ins and names may stand ("Okay, well, thanks, Sam, you ..."); after it only parting words, a name, a thank-you, and for "see you" a day or the polls; and the line does not end on a question. For her own line "I have to get back inside" counts the same way; a canvasser who mentions it, or a line about something else going back in, does not. Her first reply never closes the door after the fact. *Enforced:* `farewellIssue` and `saidBye` in worker.js (174-8); `tests/issues.test.mjs` with two reviews' lines in `tests/issue-lines.mjs`: 110 goodbyes are read, 365 lines that only contain a goodbye phrase are not, and 26 goodbyes the rule does not read are pinned as known misses.
18. **What she can't afford is not the model refusing (issue doors).** Main's character check takes "I can't write / continue / give / provide ..." for the model stepping out of its part, throws the line away and closes the door. Sarah and Linda say such things about money, forms and their vote: "I can't give you a yes tonight." "I can't write a check that big." "They want proof of income and I can't produce it." "I can't give an answer after one conversation." On an issue door those words count only when they are plainly about the conversation: "I can't generate a response," "I can't continue this role-play," "I can't give that kind of response," "I can't continue as Sarah," "I can't continue here." A bare "I can't continue." is let through as hers. Everything else in main's check is read as it is. *Enforced:* `outOfCharacterIssue` in worker.js (174-8); `tests/issues.test.mjs` (45 lines where the model leaves its part are caught, 148 of her own lines are kept, 9 bare lines are let through and pinned).
19. **"Check this" quotes the words that carry the wrong claim (issue doors).** A canvasser's line there is often two or three sentences; the card used to quote the start of the line, which could be a sentence said right. A period inside "$6.01," "6.2%" or "U.S." does not end a sentence, a claim the coach quotes across two sentences keeps both, and speech text with no periods is cut to the 150 characters that hold the claim. A line of 160 characters or fewer is quoted whole, as on every door. *Enforced:* `quoteOf` in `cleanCoach` (174-8); `tests/issues.test.mjs`.
20. **A voter sheet names only voices the server can speak.** A wrong name would fail at her door and switch streamed audio off for every other door too, so the server refuses to start with one. *Enforced:* the check after `VOICES` in worker.js (174-4).
21. **Asked if she is an AI, an issue voter never answers with her name as a no.** "No, I'm Sarah." is swapped for the honest line on Sarah's door; the name is checked on her own door only, so Kevin's and Sofia's doors read the answer exactly as main does. *Enforced:* `deniesAI` in worker.js (174-5).

## On screen

10. **A FaceTime call, not a transcript (Robert, Nov 15).** The voter fills the screen edge to edge, the button floats over the picture, and only the latest lines show as captions (on a short phone, only the voter's). Her face sits in the top third of the picture and the captions sit over her chest, never her face (Robert, Nov 16: "We want to be able to see their face there moving"). Every voter has a normal upper body, square shoulders, arms at the sides and a neckline, not a long rounded tube (Robert, Nov 16, version 171). *Enforced:* versions 168, 169, 170 and 171 (the app pages; version 170 draws a taller doorway on the conversation screen); test165.cjs checks the layout.
