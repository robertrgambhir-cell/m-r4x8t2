# How Warmup's practice voters talk: the rules

Every rule here came from Robert testing the app on his phone. Each one says what went wrong, what the voter does now and where the rule is enforced, so it can't quietly come undone. Read this before changing a voter's instructions (ai/scenarios.js), the voter's server code (ai/worker.js) or the door screens.

**How a new piece of feedback gets saved (Robert, Nov 16, 2026):**
1. **Enforce it in code when you can.** A rule in the server's code holds every time; a rule only in the AI's instructions can drift.
2. **Replay it.** Add the exact conversation Robert flagged to `tests/voter-replays.mjs`, with the check it must pass. A unit test with a stand-in model goes in `tests/turn.test.mjs` when the rule is in code.
3. **Write it here** in plain words, with his before and after.
4. **Check the live voter.** Run `WARMUP_AI_URL=https://warmup-ai.robertrgambhir.workers.dev node tests/voter-replays.mjs 3` after any change to a voter. A scheduled task runs it every week, so a change in the AI model itself gets caught too.

## The bar for every practice door

- **All three, always (Robert, Nov 15):** the voter answers at once or close to it, over a real back-and-forth, and to what the canvasser actually said. "Boy, it sure is sunny today" gets a reply about the weather, not "Who are you with?" *Enforced:* the streamed voter (/turn), the 1 s pause rule on the page, smallTalk in worker.js (small talk always gets a reply before the voter's question), the live replays.

## Lesson 1 (Sofia's 1st intro and Kevin's 2nd intro)

1. **The two intros are the same experience, graded the same way (Robert, Nov 15).** A full back-and-forth at a door both times, graded on the same four parts. *Enforced:* sofia_intro copies kevin_intro's rubric, facts and wrong claims (scenarios.js).
2. **A friendly first door, not a test of everything (Robert, Nov 15).** Short, plain lines that are easy to answer. Before: "I'm planning to vote for her Tuesday morning, same as always" (sounds like Maya ran before). After: "Oh, Maya Kaur! Yes, I'm voting for her." *Enforced:* the voters' reactions (scenarios.js); the replays check line length and "same as always."
3. **Never ask what the canvasser couldn't know (Robert, Nov 15).** Before: "What time do the polls open Tuesday?" After: no questions about polling hours, places or Maya's positions. "Anything else?" gets "No, I'm all set, thanks for volunteering." *Enforced:* scenarios.js; the replays check for poll questions.
4. **Ask only for a part that's really missing, and ask it plainly (Robert, Nov 15 and 16).** The server works out which parts were said (name, Maya Kaur, why they came) and writes the question itself; the AI adds only a few words of reaction, dropped if odd. Before: he said "the election" and Sofia asked "What's this about?"; then "Oh, hi Robert. Yes, I know about Tuesday. Oh, nice. Who are you volunteering with?"; then, asked twice, "Sorry, I'm a bit confused by what you're after. Your name first? Then you're volunteering for someone?" After: "Oh, hi there. Sorry, and you are?", then once more "Sorry, I didn't catch your name.", then she moves on to the next missing part. She only knows what she has been told (Robert, Nov 17): "Hi, I'm Robert, um, I'm" got "Who are you volunteering with?" though he never said he was volunteering. Now a name and nothing else gets "What can I do for you?", and "volunteering" is in her question only once the canvasser has said it ("And who are you with?" otherwise). *Enforced:* introParts, introPlan and cleanReaction in worker.js; turn.test.mjs; the replays.
5. **At most four of the canvasser's lines; the voter wraps up once the four parts are done (Robert, Nov 15).** *Enforced:* wrapAt 3 and hardStop 4 (scenarios.js), the page's cap.

## Every door

6. **A goodbye ends the door (Robert, Nov 15).** When the voter says goodbye, the door closes and grading starts; the canvasser gets no extra turn. A line that arrives after her goodbye gets "Bye now!" with no AI call. The canvasser's goodbye works the same way: she says goodbye back and the door closes, even if a part of the introduction is still missing (165-10 and 165-11; before, she answered "Thanks, bye now!" with "You too, bye! And what brings you by?"). *Enforced:* FAREWELL and saidBye in worker.js, and the missing-part question is skipped after a goodbye; turn.test.mjs; the replays.
7. **The voter never steps out of character (Robert, Nov 15).** Before (after his "You too goodnight"): "I can't write Sofia's next line in this exchange, because the conversation already reached its goodbye..." After: any line that talks about lines, turns, the conversation, practicing or being an AI is never shown or spoken, and the door ends with a plain goodbye. *Enforced:* outOfCharacter in worker.js; turn.test.mjs; the replays.
8. **The goodbye isn't the canvasser's to miss when the voter closed the door (Nov 15).** *Enforced:* the close item's definition (scenarios.js).
9. **Spoken words get punctuation, never changed words (Robert, Nov 15).** *Enforced:* /tidy and sameWords in worker.js; turn.test.mjs.

4b. **Answer the volunteer's question before asking hers (Robert, Nov 17).** Before: "Hi, I'm Robert, um, I'm volunteering, do you know that we have an election here next Tuesday" got "Oh, hi Robert! Who are you volunteering with?" After: "Oh, hi Robert! Yes, I know about Tuesday. Who are you volunteering with?" Lesson 1's voters are a little easier than real ones on purpose, so volunteers stay engaged. *Enforced:* smallTalk in worker.js (questions about the election, voting plans and "any questions?" are answered by the server if the AI skipped them; a greeting keeps its place); turn.test.mjs; the replays.

10b. **Wait for the volunteer; nervous people pause (Robert, Nov 17).** A line that sounds finished sends after 1.2 s of quiet; a line that stops mid-thought (ends on "um", "I'm", "and", "with" and the like, or is under three words) waits 2.8 s. Before: "Hi, I'm Robert, um, I'm" went to Sofia after 1 s. *Enforced:* waiPause in the app pages (version 172); test165.cjs.

## On screen

10. **A FaceTime call, not a transcript (Robert, Nov 15).** The voter fills the screen edge to edge, the button floats over the picture, and only the latest lines show as captions (on a short phone, only the voter's). Her face sits in the top third of the picture and the captions sit over her chest, never her face (Robert, Nov 16: "We want to be able to see their face there moving"). Every voter has a normal upper body, square shoulders, arms at the sides and a neckline, not a long rounded tube (Robert, Nov 16, version 171). *Enforced:* versions 168, 169, 170 and 171 (the app pages; version 170 draws a taller doorway on the conversation screen); test165.cjs checks the layout.

## The signature course's passersby (ai/sig/grade.js)

11. **Nobody answers a question that wasn't asked (Robert, Nov 20).** A passerby says they're registered, or that they aren't, only after the canvasser asks. One who has only heard what the petition is for says "Oh, for the schools? What do you need?" (since the signature course's version 13, Nov 23, 2026, when its measure became Prop 3; before, "Oh, the library? What do you need?"), answers the registered question if it comes next and walks on if it doesn't. Before: Ben, Soo-jin and Luis could say "I'm registered" when nobody had asked (Sig 2.1 and 2.6). After: the line above, for lesson 2's four passersby and the woman in scrubs in lessons 1 and 3. A passerby who asked "What's this?" stays to hear the answer, and the woman in scrubs says "Me? I vote in every election." only to a question about voting; to anything else she waits (Nov 20 review). *Enforced:* the "lib" branch in talk() for 2.ben, 2.stop, 2.kayla, 1.marisol and 3.marisol (ai/sig/grade.js); sig-grade.test.mjs replays his cases and the new sigscripts path "lib" walks it in the browser. Playbook rule 37b.

12. **A passerby keeps walking (Robert, Nov 20, 8:23 AM).** "In an actual interaction, unlike at the doors where they're kind of like a sitting duck, this person is walking, and they keep walking. They don't just totally stop while you pause." In lesson 2's tries a passerby walks up in view while the coach talks and keeps walking once the canvasser's mic is on. They slow while the canvasser talks, stop when the opener lands ("stop," "lib" or "not") and leave after a goodbye or "time." Every line still waits for the canvasser's pause (rule 34 of the playbook) and is heard before anything else, so an opener that's been said always gets its answer; only the walking doesn't wait. If they get past a silent canvasser, they say "Oh, sorry, I'm busy. Have a good day." while still in view and walk on, and the try misses part 4 ("Took so long they walked on."). Someone who stopped and is left waiting says "Okay, bye now." and goes (Nov 20 review). Before: they walked in and stood still until the talk ended. Dwayne's "Nope. Never got around to it." is now "No, I'm not." and every passerby's "I'd rather decide for myself." is "I'll think about it." (playbook rule 39c). *Enforced:* walker() and brushOff() in ai/sig/engine2.js, walkedOn() in ai/sig/grade.js; sig-grade.test.mjs checks the grading and the lines, and ai/tests/sig-walkpast.cjs walks a silent try, a prompt opener and a silent 2nd try in the browser. Playbook B3.

13. **Nobody agrees to what they weren't asked, and every reply answers what was just said (Robert, Nov 21, 12:56 PM; playbook rule 37e).** "I asked her, 'Are you registered to vote in Los Angeles County, and are you a lover of libraries?' She didn't answer. She said, 'Sure, I'll sign.' I didn't ask her to sign anything." Before: told what it's for and asked the registered question, Soo-jin said "Oh, that's nice. Sure, I'll sign." After: she answers the question first ("Yes, I am."), and with no word yet on signatures or a petition she asks "Oh, the library? What do you need?"; "Sure, I'll sign" comes only once signing has come up. *Enforced:* grade.js (lesson 2's passersby check `sig`, signatures or a petition said, before "sure"); his exact words are a replay in tests/sig-grade.test.mjs ("L2 Soo-jin answers Robert's question, asks what he needs and signs once asked"), with the expected replies of every lesson 2 replay updated.

14. **Passersby reply live; the grader decides what they do (Robert, Nov 21, 2:11 PM; playbook rule 37f).** His idea at 12:56 PM: "have the AI say whatever it thinks it would naturally say without it knowing how it's supposed to react ... At the same time, it gets an ever-growing list of things it can't say." His pick: "Build live replies next. I agree with your one change: whether the passerby stops or keeps walking stays tied to how good my opener was." Before: every reply was one of a few scripted lines. After: the grader still picks the reply (stop, ask what this is, sign, leave), and the AI server writes the passerby's words to what the learner actually said, in their own ElevenLabs voice. *Enforced:* POST /sig/reply in worker.js: only the five lessons' passersby and only their decided replies; the model gets who they are, the talk so far, the decision and the gist, never the lesson's parts; SIG_NEVER is the list of things a passerby never says (add each new note there); sigLiveCheck sends back {fallback} when the words would sign when signing wasn't decided, drop a decided signature, flip or volunteer whether they're registered, use a pet name, step out of character or run long, and the page then plays the scripted line, as it does when the live line takes over 4.5 s. Tests: tests/sig-live.test.mjs (stubbed) and tests/sig-live-replay.mjs (Robert's exchanges against the live server).

15. **Nobody is cut off mid-sentence (Robert, Nov 22, 2026, 4:10 PM; playbook rule 34a).** "It cut me off in lesson 2, partway through, in the 'your first try' ... it just cut off my voice. I was still talking, unfinished." The server's log had his second line as his phone heard it: "We're collecting signatures on Uh have something on the ballot in November to get". It was taken as finished after about a second of quiet, because "get" wasn't on the list of words that need more after them, and the man left: "Sorry, I've got to run, good luck!" Before: a line sent 1.2 seconds after its last word unless that word was on a short list ("um," "and," "to"), and when the phone stopped listening on its own partway through (iOS does, after a short pause), the app sent what it had at once. After: a line that could go on waits 2.8 seconds: one that ends on a word needing more after it ("to get," "to keep," "a ballot measure," "a new state"), and when unsure it waits; only "to sign," "to help" and the like end a thought there. A line that ends finished still gets the quick answer: a greeting ("Hi, how's it going?"), a goodbye or thanks, an ask to sign, or a question the phone heard end with "?". When the phone stops listening, the app listens again and keeps the words it heard, so a line ends on the quiet rule, a tap on "I'm done" or, at most, a minute's talk; a microphone error still sends what was heard, and a walker held by those words is let go. *Enforced:* `pauseMs` (MID, MORE, TO_DONE and DONE_END) in ai/sig/grade.js and `listen()` in ai/sig/engine1.js. *Replayed:* his exact line in tests/sig-grade.test.mjs, and tests/sig-cutoff.cjs plays it in a browser with a 1.7-second pause after "to get", then the phone stopping mid-line, and checks each line is heard whole, once. The door-knocking lessons' own listener has the same short list (`WAI_MID` in wdpfxqlsvp/app.html); its fix waits for "ship it."
